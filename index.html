Brief History of Malolos

1225

The Ancient Port of Li-Han

Pre-colonial Malolos thrives as an important maritime trading post.

1571

Spanish Conquest and Encomienda

Spanish forces take control and establish the area as an encomienda.

1580

Official Founding of the Town

Augustinian friars officially establish Malolos as a civil town.

1888

Petition of the Women of Malolos

Twenty prominent local women successfully petition for educational rights.

1892

Manila-Dagupan Railway Opening

The new railway system connects Malolos to Manila, boosting trade.

1898

The Historic Malolos Congress

Revolutionary delegates meet at Barasoain Church to draft a constitution.

1899

First Philippine Republic Inaugurated

Malolos becomes Asia's first democratic republican capital under President Aguinaldo.

1901

Provincial Capital of Bulacan

The administrative center of Bulacan province officially shifts to Malolos.

1904

Territorial Expansion and Mergers

Nearby Barasoain and Santa Isabel municipalities merge into Malolos.

1945

Liberation of the Town

Allied forces and local Filipino guerrillas successfully secure the town.

1998

Centennial Presidential Inauguration

President Joseph Estrada holds his inaugural oath-taking at Barasoain Church.

2001

National Heritage Landmark Declaration

The historic downtown area gains official designation as a heritage zone.

2002

Official Ratification of Cityhood

Malolos officially transitions from a municipality into a component city.

2010

Annual Cityhood Day Ordinance

The local government permanently declares December 18 as Cityhood Day.

2024

Regional Economic Outperformance

The city hits a 7.8% GDP growth rate, exceeding regional averages.

2026

127th Republic Anniversary and Modern Hub

Malolos celebrates its rich history while growing as a top regional center.

Cradle of the Republic

Malolos hosted the Malolos Congress and the inauguration of the First Philippine Republic, earning its place as the cradle of the Philippine Republic.

Heritage Town Center

The historic town center is a National Historical Landmark, preserving the churches, ancestral homes, and civic spaces connected to Malolos’s history.

Origin of the Name

One popular theory traces “Malolos” to the Tagalog word paluslos (“downward”). Locals describing the river’s downward flow were reportedly misunderstood by Spanish priests asking for the place name, and paluslos became “Malolos.”
